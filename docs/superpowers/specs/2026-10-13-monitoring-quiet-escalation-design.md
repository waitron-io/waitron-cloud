# Quiet periods and one venue reminder

You should see an incident in the portal as soon as the monitor observes it, while a short blip can end without sending email. D59 adds an optional wait before opening mail and one optional still-open reminder to the same recipients. Both settings default to off. Existing incident detection thresholds, account mail and the venue email relay keep their separate rules.

This is a proposed local mechanism, based on `d681db284ac8babeffb9f1029c2c32f8dc47701c`, whose manifest ends at migration 036. File references below are **design evidence, never runtime proof**. The later implementation must supply executed test and receipt evidence. Lane D's unattended authorization settles the minor choices below; no interactive approval or per-task agents are required. A separate fresh-context docs review and docs-only landing precede one cohesive implementation PR.

## Scope and chosen defaults

- Add `MonitorConfig.quietSeconds?: number` and `reminderSeconds?: number`, exposed as `CLOUD_MONITOR_QUIET_SECONDS` and `CLOUD_MONITOR_REMINDER_SECONDS`. Missing or `0` disables that option independently. Accept only finite integer seconds from 0 through 604800; reject blank, negative, fractional, nonnumeric, infinite and larger values. Seven days is a technical input bound, not a recommended production duration.
- Capture both values once per new episode. Quiet time starts at its database `opened_at`; reminder time starts separately for each recipient at the opening outbox row's first committed `sent.finished_at`. Persist absolute deadlines; restart, retry and subsequent configuration changes never move them.
- One reminder queue identity per episode and recipient, with ordinary bounded transport retries. No repeat escalation, on-call routing, additional recipients, portal settings, API response or UI changes.
- The existing account opt-in and current EN/ES language apply. Snapshot eligible **account IDs** at opening, not a list recomputed when the delay ends. An owner added or an account enabled mid-episode receives neither its historical opening nor its reminder/recovery. Resolve the current verified address at enqueue; require that address still matches the encrypted message at handoff.
- Use PostgreSQL time for boundaries, including exact equality. A due deadline is eligibility, not a delivery-time promise. Local examples/tests use synthetic short delays only. Production reliability objectives, thresholds, provider choice, live deployment and retention policy remain owner decisions under `docs/launch-evidence.md:131` and gate 9 at `:161`.

## Durable episode and notification identity

An **episode** is one uninterrupted open period. Keep `venue_incidents.id` and revision semantics intact. Add `venue_alert_episodes` with UUID `id`, `incident_id`, unique `(incident_id,opening_revision)`, `installation_id`, `opening_revision`, `opened_at`, captured `quiet_seconds`/`reminder_seconds`, `opening_due_at`, nullable `opening_processed_at`, nullable `recovery_revision`, `status` (`open/recovered/suppressed`), `legacy`, and `recovery_complete`. A later reopening makes a new UUID even though the incident row is reused. Record recovery's actual incident revision. A reminder never increments it or changes `changed_at`.

Add `venue_alert_recipients`, keyed by `(episode_id,account_id)`, containing nullable durable `reminder_due_at` and reminder/recovery slot outcomes (`waiting/queued/cancelled/done`). A unique delivery is the durable queued outcome; never reset a terminal slot to retry a historical notification. Disabled reminders start cancelled; recovery starts waiting until closure determines eligibility. Enforce positive opening/recovery revisions, bounded captured seconds and valid deadline/status combinations in SQL. Do not store raw addresses in this schedule. Snapshot creation and all outbox/delivery fan-out statements use groups of at most **100**, inside the same venue transaction.

Extend `service_alert_deliveries` with nullable `episode_id` for historical links and `notification` (`transition/reminder`, default `transition`). Replace its unique `(incident_id,revision,account_id)` with `(incident_id,revision,account_id,notification)` and also enforce one episode/account/state/notification link for non-null episode IDs. Preserve `state='open'|'recovered'`. Opening and reminder share the episode's opening revision and open state; recovery uses its recovery revision. Queuing a reminder therefore cannot invalidate an opening still queued for another recipient.

Add only optional `ServiceAlert.notification?: 'reminder'` to the encrypted payload. Missing means the existing transition. Permit it only with `state='open'`; reject null, arrays, other strings and extra keys. Preserve canonical ISO evidence rules and existing AES-GCM associated data. The delivery row's discriminator must agree with the decrypted payload. Old payloads remain readable without rewriting ciphertext. Episode identity comes from the authenticated delivery mapping; incident/venue/kind/state/revision must still agree.

## Lifecycle and concurrency

1. **Observe/open:** record the incident immediately, create its episode and eligible-recipient snapshot transactionally. With quiet disabled, enqueue opening messages in that transaction as today. Otherwise retain only durable scheduled work. No network runs under database locks.
2. **Due opening:** re-evaluate current service health and evidence before enqueue. The episode must still be the current open revision on its original non-revoked, non-superseded installation and a configured test service. Intersect its snapshot with current owner, active account, verified address and enabled preference checks. Queue once, marking `opening_processed_at` even for an empty eligible set so later opt-ins cannot create historical mail. Any failed insert rolls back the entire fan-out and markers.
3. **Due reminder:** only a committed opening delivery with `email_outbox.status='sent'` qualifies that recipient. Set `reminder_due_at` once from its `finished_at` plus captured seconds. A pending, processing, failed, expired or cancelled opening creates no reminder. At the deadline, re-evaluate the still-open incident and current authority/evidence, then queue its single reminder or cancel its slot. Use a distinct outbox ID and the same opening revision. Keep expiry at 24 hours from enqueue, attempts at 8 and existing claim/retry behavior.
4. **Recover:** close the episode and cancel unsent opening/reminder eligibility immediately. Queue recovery only for snapshot recipients with a sent opening for this episode and current permissions. Reconcile on subsequent scans even when the incident remains recovered: an opening already handed to transport may commit `sent` after recovery was observed. That late receipt can create the missing recovery exactly once, while the incident is still at this episode's recovery revision. Never send an obsolete recovery after suppression or a new opening.
5. **Suppress:** no recovery notice. Cancel every future slot and pending outbox notice; a processing claim retains its truthful outcome and fails the live gate if not yet handed off. Do not clear a live processing claim to cancel an in-flight send. Installation/service removal and replacement cannot revive this episode's schedule when service returns. New failure after suppression creates a new episode. Recipient opt-out or lost ownership at a due decision cancels that slot permanently; send-time revocation cancels its queued message. Current eligibility cannot authorize a newly added snapshot member.

Reconcile within `MonitorWorker.runOnce()` after the observation, including unchanged recovered states. Keep one venue row `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and the current installation `FOR SHARE ... SKIP LOCKED`; process other venues when either is locked. Use a consistent order: monitor, installation, incident, episode, then recipient/delivery rows, respecting replacement's existing order and skip behavior. No new email-completion lock of a monitor/installation is needed: it commits only its current claimed outbox result; the next monitor reads the durable receipt. Deadlock/insert failure rolls back, retains the due work and uses the existing 30-second retry. Schedule `next_check_at` for the earlier of the usual minute and a known future deadline; poll late sent receipts at the normal observation cadence. Do not spin on an overdue but blocked/settled slot.

At both delayed enqueue and handoff preserve every existing permission/service gate. At handoff require current claim ID, processing status, unexpired lease/message, payload/discriminator mapping, incident ID/revision/venue/kind/state, current owner, active account, current verified matching email, enabled preference, test environment, current original installation, configured service and required route placement/hostname. Refresh language at send. For reminder **and recovery**, additionally require the sent opening of the same episode/account. Keep the final recheck after database waits. Recovery must not bypass a sent or permission check to solve the late-receipt race.

**Told** means the opening's outbox record has committed `status='sent'` under its valid claim. SMTP metadata is optional today, including local mail and accepted sends with malformed metadata; do not require `message_id` or reinterpret provider acceptance as inbox delivery. An uncertain SMTP acceptance or crash before the sent commit can cause retries/duplicates, or leave a person who received mail without a recorded opening. Do not infer that they were told or send them speculative recovery. A stale claim cannot write a receipt. These unavoidable limits also apply to the reminder; “one” means one durable notification identity, not exactly-once SMTP.

Due enqueue uses current monitor-derived evidence, not the observation saved when the episode opened: route failures require canonical `checkedAt`; stale-probe evidence may omit it; certificate notices require both canonical `checkedAt` and `certificateNotAfter`; contact/snapshot alerts carry neither. If fresh evaluation recovers or suppresses the issue, close/cancel it instead of producing an invalid or stale opening/reminder.

Cleanup must protect all opening receipts needed by a pending reminder/recovery or queued recovery handoff, including a recovered episode with a still-processing opening. Mark `recovery_complete` only after every opening has a terminal outcome and each eligible recovery slot has settled. Once all dependent mail is terminal and metered, delete links/recipient/episode history with the existing explicit email-history cutoff and bounded transactions. No automatic retention duration is introduced.
