# Proposed address suggestions for Spain

**Status: PROPOSED, 2026-10-16.** This design is for owner review and queued implementation. Landing these documents approves neither a release nor changes to provider account caps. Google is the proposed first provider, with Geoapify as a separately gated fallback. Manual entry works at all times, without Cloud login or allowance. Cloud owns this provider and allowance contract; Waitron owns its setup form, country-pack checks and local server. No Waitron changes are authorised by this document.

You can ask for suggestions while setting up a Spanish venue, review an editable address, and explicitly confirm it. Suggestions never silently replace your fields. This adds no map, subscription requirement, general geocoding service or address database. Fiscal allocations, hash chains, golden records and issued invoices remain outside scope.

## Storage and display contract

The official public sources supplied for this design were fetched on 2026-10-16. EEA applicability follows the customer's **billing address**, not the venue's Spanish location. [EEA service terms B1.4](https://cloud.google.com/terms/maps-platform/eea/maps-service-terms) and [non-EEA B1.3.2](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms) permit listed Validation address fields for downstream transactions for 30 days, followed by deletion or replacement with End User data supplied through confirmation or correction.

The proposed interpretation is that a separate, explicit confirmation of the whole address after Address Validation supplies that replacement. The scope note gives the source wording and Google’s published accepted-address example. Owner acceptance of the interpretation is a release gate. A Places list click alone does not establish durable storage rights. [EEA permitted uses](https://cloud.google.com/terms/maps-platform/eea-places-api-permitted-uses) permit address autocomplete but do not establish permanent Places storage. The EEA Geocoding exception is not permission for Places or Validation.

Keep original manual input separate from transient provider suggestions. Google selection opens a Validation draft; it does not save a merchant record. The person reviews every mapped field, makes any corrections, and confirms the whole address. Only those confirmed or corrected fields become the person's merchant record in Waitron. Coordinates, confidence, validation flags, place IDs and raw replies do not become merchant data through that confirmation. Cloud retains no address payload or address-derived fingerprint.

Provider content exists only in request memory and active display memory. There is no cross-request or cross-provider address cache, persistent queue, browser storage or backup of drafts. Discard it on cancel, expiry, provider switch and page exit. A lost response or worker restart may require a fresh workflow within the limits; never replay a provider call automatically. The proposed workflow lifetime is at most 15 minutes, not a vendor cache permission. Thirty days is an upper permission, not the proposed cache lifetime.

Show official Google Maps attribution beside both the Google suggestion list and Validation draft, following the [Places](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies) and [Validation](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/address-validation/policies) policies. Keep branding readable in English and Spanish, light and dark, at 390 and 1280 pixels. Keep required third-party attribution. Geoapify results have their own distinct region with OpenStreetMap attribution always and Geoapify attribution on Free. Never relabel Google content as Geoapify, merge lists or blend address components. This flow has no map. Public privacy and terms links incorporating required Google references must be available before enablement.

## Account authority and allowance

Propose ten completed, confirmed selected addresses per Cloud account per UTC calendar month, shared across its venues and both providers, without a paid subscription. Show `used`, `pending`, `remaining` and `resetAt`; remaining excludes pending reservations. Manual entry consumes nothing. Autocomplete has a separate abuse ceiling of 100 calls per account per UTC month and 20 per UTC day across both providers.

Before pairing, an unpaired setup key can create only a bounded lookup approval request. It cannot call a provider. Through Cloud login, a verified account explicitly approves an expiring lookup-only grant bound to that key, local venue, environment and workflow. Proposed default authority is an organisation owner who also has `can_register_venues` for the chosen organisation/legal business. This does not pair or register the venue. After pairing, require a live installation lease and the explicit account, venue and workflow grant; installation authority alone cannot spend the person's balance. There is no silent organisation-wide sharing.

Use a single workflow/operation UUID for one selected address and its terminal receipt, with at most one operation per grant. Bind grants to the approving session and expire them on session expiry/revocation, account disablement, loss of owner/registration authority, venue mismatch or installation revocation/replacement. Recheck authority under lock before reservation and confirmation, including after lock waits. Both pre-pairing and paired flows remain blocked until the Waitron grant bridge is independently implemented and proved.

Reserve one pending customer selection before opening a provider workflow. In PostgreSQL, serialize by account and UTC period so `used + pending <= 10` across workers and venues, including the first row in a period. On successful explicit confirmation, debit once by operation identity and return a nonaddress receipt, meaning only identifiers, counts and outcome. Release the customer reservation on known failure, cancellation or expiry. A confirmation racing cancellation/expiry has exactly one terminal outcome. Status resolves lost confirmation responses without another debit; conflicting reuse is refused. Expire a workflow by the earlier of 15 minutes or its customer-month boundary, so a pending selection cannot cross that reset.

## Provider limits and zero paid usage

The [session pricing source](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/session-pricing) explicitly bills a session ending in Address Validation as **Address Validation Enterprise**. Its [list price](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing) has 1,000 free validations per month, then USD 25 per 1,000. The underlying Pro listing of 5,000 free does not price this flow. Validly terminated autocomplete is free, while abandoned, missing or reused tokens incur Autocomplete Requests, with 10,000 free per month. Count **every** autocomplete as potentially paid instead of relying on session discounts.

These proposed ceilings are aggregate TOTAL consumption, including existing usage, verified reservations for unrelated usage and in-flight work, not an additional pool:

| Provider unit | Monthly total | Daily total | Other limits |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Google Validation Enterprise | 500 | 50 | Reserve one termination slot before opening a Google session; at most one validation per workflow. |
| Google autocomplete | 4,000 | 400 | At most eight Google autocomplete calls per workflow. |
| Geoapify requests | 10,000 | 1,000 | At most one automatic fallback request per workflow. |
| All provider requests | Within each ceiling | Within each ceiling | Shared two requests per second, enforced as at least 500 ms between dispatches across workers. |

Hard paid usage budget is **USD 0**. Account-wide free usage includes every project/key on the shared allowance. Account for all such usage or reserve verified upper bounds for it; unknown or stale information disables the affected provider. These are proposed defaults, not production qualification. The owner's existing key caps and alerts are unset and remain unchanged. [Cloud Billing budgets are alerts](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/manage-costs), not hard spending caps.

Commit quota reservations in PostgreSQL before any network dispatch, using a consistent lock order and database time. Each attempt has a unique nonaddress identity and durable reserved/dispatched/settled state. Lock the operator switch and relevant counter rows before dispatch authorization. Recheck freshness, authority, expiry and daily/monthly capacity. Network dispatch never happens inside a long database transaction. A committed dispatch authorization consumes the provider unit even if the process crashes before sending. Uncertain or timed-out requests retain consumed units; never blindly retry or refund them. Release only capacity proved not dispatched, including unused termination holds after abandonment. Customer selection release never refunds provider consumption.

Google’s [pay-as-you-go documentation](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pay-as-you-go), read 2026-10-16, resets free usage on the first of the month at midnight US Pacific time. Use `America/Los_Angeles`, including daylight saving, for Google provider-month and provider-day counters; UTC is only the separate customer allowance calendar. Geoapify’s reset timezone must be verified before fallback activation. Fence admission around provider resets until old in-flight work is accounted for. Where an attempt could count in either period, reserve it against both until reconciled. Do not reset an uncertain request out of the budget at midnight. Carry ambiguous Geoapify attempts against both possible periods until its billing boundary is verified. Quota-store failure, stale counters, missing shared-usage bounds or operator disablement refuse new provider dispatches. Disablement fences later authorizations; already authorized requests stay counted and may finish.

Issue one server-created Google session token bound to account and workflow. Persist only its nonaddress binding and lifecycle; never reuse it after validation, expiry, abandonment or provider switch. The eight-call bound and termination reservation apply even when no suggestion is selected. Reaching a workflow limit offers manual entry; it does not create another automatic Google session.

## Failure, fallback and editable fields

For Google unavailability, timeout, 429 or budget refusal, propose one new Geoapify EU autocomplete request on the **original user query**, with its own reservation. Do not race providers in the background, retry automatically, or send Google's returned address or place ID to Geoapify. Cancel or ignore stale responses by workflow generation when text changes or a provider switches. Preserve manual text throughout. Exhausted account allowance or abuse limits go directly to manual entry, without bypass through fallback.

Fallback remains manual-only until Geoapify commercial Free eligibility, durable merchant-storage/licensing and EU endpoint processing terms are confirmed, as the scope note still requires. No optional geocode is included; adding one requires a separate reserved request inside all limits and owner review of the changed scope. These documents do not qualify Geoapify for production.

Propose only `addressLine1`, optional `addressLine2`, `postalCode`, `city`, `province` and matching `countryCode: ES`. Preserve manually entered units and other manual fields unless the person explicitly chooses their replacement. A single provider supplies each draft. Waitron's country pack resolves canonical province and checks postcode/province compatibility; unknown or foreign country, conflicting province or unsupported fiscal area cannot become an accepted complete draft. Any province inferred from a returned postcode must be disclosed for review. A filter is not returned country evidence. Never fill missing values from an expected benchmark answer.

Waitron decides whether city means village or municipality, shows any ambiguity for correction, and runs its existing client/server validation, fiscal-country-pack checks and time-zone checks. Provider output cannot set fiscal territory or time zone. The measured 48/60 Google complete-list result does not meet the scope note's proposed 95% gate and proves neither a first-result guarantee nor acceptance by the real setup journey. The owner must settle locality and quality acceptance before release.

## Proposed Cloud contract and handoff

Add account routes `/api/account/address-lookup/{inspect,approve,revoke}` as POST with session, origin and CSRF checks, and GET `/api/account/address-lookup/balance`. Add POST `/v1/address-lookup/{request,start,status,autocomplete,validate,confirm,cancel}` with a dedicated signed, action-specific lookup proof. Only `request` can precede account approval; it creates expiring metadata with a pairing-style confirmation code and makes no provider call. All later actions bind the grant, operation, monotonic request sequence and setup key or paired installation/lease. Sign the complete canonical action body, including the original query, selected text, confirmation nonce and whole-address acknowledgement wherever accepted, as well as all identifiers and binding metadata. Verify the signature before using any field; changing any accepted field under an unchanged signature must refuse. Keep signed address bytes transient and out of receipts, fingerprints and logs. Do not broaden existing pairing proofs or persist payloads through `installation_operations`.

`start` returns operation identity, expiry and balance after customer reservation; `autocomplete` returns provider-labelled transient suggestions; `validate` accepts the chosen Google prediction transiently and returns the Validation draft. The active caller carries any selected text between requests in memory, never through a Cloud address cache. Return a random, one-use confirmation nonce with a selectable draft, storing only its hash and operation binding. `confirm` accepts that nonce and an explicit whole-address-reviewed acknowledgement from the signed caller after Waitron's checks, without address bytes. It returns only operation outcome and balance. Duplicate confirmation can return the existing terminal receipt; a repeated provider sequence never dispatches again. Manual corrections remain with the person and Waitron. `status` returns metadata only; lost drafts are not reconstructible from Cloud.

Bound original query to 512 UTF-8 bytes, responses to five candidates and 64 KiB, each call to five seconds, and one outstanding provider call per workflow. Refuse malformed bodies and unknown fields. Use `Cache-Control: no-store` for every lookup response and prohibit payload logging, telemetry capture, address hashes, persistent queues and backup capture. Retain only minimum nonaddress account/count/operation/outcome records needed for quota and duplicate protection; prune expired grants/tokens and period data after reconciliation, retaining terminal operation tombstones only until their grant and replay windows close. An expired grant can never resurrect an old operation.

Implement behind an absent-by-default injected lookup option in `createAccountApp` and `startAccountServer`. No production provider/key wiring belongs to the planned branches. The Cloud consent screen `/address-lookup` handles approval and account balance, not venue address editing. The Waitron owner must supply the signed setup bridge, form mapping, validation, whole-address confirmation and a joint local proof. The scope note records the terms provenance and the limits of the retained CartoCiudad audit; no new measurement is part of this plan.

Release gates remain owner acceptance of this interpretation and defaults; applicable billing terms, verified Geoapify reset timezone and shared-usage accounting; public privacy/terms and attribution; Geoapify eligibility/storage/EU terms for fallback; locality and quality acceptance; the Waitron handoff and joint proof; and explicit operational enablement. Keep providers disabled and manual entry usable until the relevant gates are met. Do not change provider account caps as part of landing or implementation.
