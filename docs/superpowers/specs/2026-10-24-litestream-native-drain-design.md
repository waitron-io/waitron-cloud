**Reject `/stop` followed by passive stack polling as the repair.** After a successful stop, a complete inventory of known store monitors at their timer selects could establish a stable property: later cycles skip the closed DB, provided nothing reopens it. Non-atomic sampling alone does not disprove that narrower argument. However, polling begins after DB close has already cancelled the replica context. Source-based inference: an accepted replica exchange can be interrupted before the proposed barrier exists; a later idle stack cannot recover its accounting. An earlier `transport.whenIdle` has the already-proved late-admission race. This rejects this sequence's full-session guarantee, not every conceivable technique on the official binary.

Fresh experiment attempt: both `node --test R/control-drain-20261024/native-control-stop.test.mjs` and its `D152_NATIVE_CONTROL=1` control refused at loopback `listen EPERM`, before launching Litestream. The two generated scratch directories were collected. No new runtime observation or PUT-cancellation reproduction is claimed. The stack-producing probe was not rerun because it overwrites campaign receipts. No Go compiler is on PATH; Docker remains unavailable until 3 November.

Idle waits, quiet periods, `/stop` replies, shutdown-request filters, accepting interrupted evidence, and replacing continuous replication with `-once` do not satisfy this contract. Choose a minimal native graceful-drain extension, qualified separately before any adoption.

## Native extension contract

Introduce an opt-in, instance-wide `Store.Drain(ctx) error`, exposed only through the existing private Unix socket as `POST /drain`. Support the smoke's single explicit S3 DB, ordinary continuous replication and existing compaction/snapshot/L0-retention settings. Refuse unsupported directory watching, MCP, heartbeat, validation, additional DBs or other storage producers before claiming support. Existing behavior outside the opt-in path stays unchanged.

Use a terminal lifecycle `running, then draining, then drained` or `failed`. Entering `draining` atomically closes admission of **background cycles**, including lazy replica starts and control operations that can create storage work. Admission and lifecycle checks use the same synchronization; a timer selected concurrently with drain cannot start a new cycle. Do not gate individual SDK requests: retries, pagination, retention and other follow-up requests inside an admitted cycle must finish normally.

Wake idle DB, replica and store monitors through a separate graceful-stop channel. Let active cycles finish with their original, uncancelled contexts, and join every monitor and its owned I/O before closing the DB. Cover DB initialization that starts the replica while a cycle is already running; no goroutine may appear after the join boundary. Then, with background admission still closed, the drain owner performs the normal final WAL/replica flush and DB close. Preserve retention settings and all work belonging to accepted cycles. Do not cancel producer contexts on the success path until they can no longer have storage I/O.

The existing `DB.Close` cannot simply be called first. Its reuse is permitted only after these joins and after proving final flush cannot restart a monitor. Store/process close after successful drain is idempotent and performs no further storage work. While draining or drained, reject every other control request that could touch storage or reopen the DB. Repeated drain observes the same terminal result; concurrent calls share one drain operation. Collect errors from accepted work and final flush; no swallowed close error or process exit code can manufacture success.

Request: `{protocol:"litestream-drain-v1", nonce:<caller token>, timeoutMs:<remaining budget>}`. Success is HTTP 200 with exactly `{protocol:"litestream-drain-v1", status:"drained", nonce:<same token>, build:<approved build identity>}`. It means all native producers are joined, final flush succeeded, DB is closed and no future storage work can start. Timeout, cancellation, interrupted I/O, unrecovered cycle failure, unsupported configuration and missing capability never produce that reply. The injected 503 and its successful SDK retry remain valid, fully counted work. A timeout may require cancelling I/O during failure cleanup; the complete session then fails its audit. Control request/reply bodies are capped at 1,024 bytes; nonce is 32 lowercase hexadecimal characters and timeoutMs is a positive integer no greater than the remaining 2,000 ms budget.

In the smoke's candidate path, retain all storage observers and audit streams, request drain, validate its bounded reply, then call `transport.whenIdle(() => stop(replicator))`. Keep producer logs open through child close. End audits, close transports, verify lifetimes/inventory and clean storage in the existing order. Native acknowledgement does not replace independent request/byte/lifetime reconciliation.

## Limits and provenance

All existing assertions, pins, retries, failure injection, phases and deadlines remain. Specifically retain one absolute 2,000 ms stop-operation budget covering drain, transport idle, SIGTERM and observed child close; do not restart it between stages. Keep the separate 2,000 ms force-stop timer, 80,000 ms child watchdog, 3,000 ms native harness deadline, 110,000 ms genuine-case deadline, 30,000 ms transport timeout, 1,500 ms delayed successful PUT replies, 40 × 500 ms upload polling and 12 restore attempts with 1,000 ms retry waits. Timeout means refusal, never extra time or partial success.

Preserve producer/observer attempt identity, retry order, both independent body counters, every phase and version lifetime, restore of two sales, integrity/fiscal checks, one injected failure, administration reconciliation, privacy and owned-resource cleanup. Include all drain-time requests and final flushes; do not reset meters, discard shutdown traffic or forgive interrupted transfers.

**Owner decision before implementation:** a patched executable changes the subject being measured. Before writing the extension, creating its pipeline or building custom artifacts, ask whether to pursue a separately pinned patched binary with eventual adoption conditional on qualification, or retain the official executable and leave this repair open. The recommended choice is to pursue the explicit native drain under the contract above. Candidate-only experimentation is a narrower approval and cannot authorize later adoption. Keep the official 0.5.17 archive pins and their existing genuine cases. After that decision, qualify the extension in additional candidate cases with a distinct build/version and manifest containing base revision, patch SHA-256, exact Go toolchain and dependencies, build command/platform, output SHA-256 and CI build receipt. Never label this executable the official pin or use it to clear unchanged-pin qualification. No actual artifact digest is known yet.

Adopting that build into the existing smoke requires the owner’s explicit resolution of the pin requirement and the resulting qualification; this design does not grant an exception. The next milestone is the owner decision, with D152’s official-pin repair still open. There is no proposed accounting-contract relaxation or product/provider change. If preserving the unchanged executable remains mandatory, report the unresolved repair rather than claiming a guarantee.

## Acceptance

Require deterministic native controls for held requests before transport admission, held committed PUT replies, chained retention lists, timer/admission races, lazy replica creation, final flush, timeout and cleanup. Remove each admission/join/acknowledgement guard in a disposable candidate and require the intended behavioral assertion to fail; restore it and require success.

Qualify the exact patched macOS artifact with real HTTP and full-session accounting, then genuine Linux sale/restore/version-history cases in CI, using the exact separately pinned artifact. Run all original official-pin genuine cases unchanged too. Neither native fake-storage success nor green stub tests substitute for Linux qualification. Save exact code/artifact identities and every head/merge CI job result before a qualification claim. The driver owns fresh-context plan review, docs-only landing, later implementation review and campaign/backlog publication.
