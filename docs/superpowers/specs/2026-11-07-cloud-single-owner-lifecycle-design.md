## Your email and strongest factor

A factor is an enrolled sign-in proof: an authenticator app, SMS or a passkey with a user check on your device. Without one, your verified email is your entire identity, as at signup; there is no personal/business identity to investigate. You must enrol a factor before connecting a venue box, enabling remote access or adding a payment method. Check every way of starting and completing these actions, including after waiting and for existing accounts. Each country has its own account; two countries mean two accounts.

With an app/passkey enrolled, sign in with password plus an app code or saved recovery code, or a verified passkey. Password changes require your strongest currently enrolled proof; reset requires your verified-mailbox link plus app, passkey or recovery code. A passkey needs no extra app. Exclude weaker SMS sign-in when an app/passkey exists: a stolen password and control of your phone number would otherwise expose account data. No safe weaker route has been demonstrated.

With SMS as your sole factor, sign-in requires password plus SMS; reset requires the mailbox link plus a code to your already enrolled number. **Proposed:** a saved recovery code can replace that SMS proof. SMS alone never resets a password. Strongest-factor checks also govern handover, connecting a box, enabling remote access, adding a payment method and factor/phone changes. A sign-in established with only email, password or SMS cannot bypass an account's current stronger proof. Ordinary password reset preserves factors.

## Hand over one organisation

You sign in with your strongest factor and name the recipient's email. They verify that address, sign in, enrol their own factor and accept the exact transfer. One database transaction replaces the sole owner and ends your organisation access together, at acceptance. You give no later confirmation. First-time recipients need signup for a country account alone; [current signup](../../../packages/registration/src/signup.ts#L71) creates an organisation/business too.

**Proposed defaults:** one pending transfer per organisation, displayed 24-hour expiry and cancellation by either participant. Pending, cancelled or expired transfers leave you owner and give the recipient no access. Changing recipient requires cancellation. Security changes cancel affected pending transfers, except the recipient's required initial enrolment, which ties their fresh proof to the transfer before acceptance. Completion rechecks exact parties, that ownership has not changed (its recorded version), country, authentication and expiry after waits; competing acceptance/cancellation has one outcome. If a reply is lost, retrying returns that same outcome; only someone currently authorised may read private results.

The [plan for country accounts and worldwide Cloud operators](../plans/2026-10-22-cloud-global-country-split.md#3-single-owner-authority-and-future-handover) requires immediate revocation of former-owner sessions, approvals, support grants, consent and queued work. Consent needs independent renewal; replies must avoid revealing account existence, with attempt limits and notifications. Recovery or replacement already underway must reach a known outcome first. Unrelated organisations and machine services with permission independent of the owner remain. Taxpayer/legal-business identity stays unchanged; a different taxpayer needs separate Waitron handling. Cloud ownership grants no Waitron staff or support role, Cloud operator role (running Cloud), fiscal authority (tax-related powers) or server-activation authority; each retains separate permission checks.

## SMS beside the app

SMS is weaker: SIM swapping can steal control of your number ([NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/protecting-sms-messages-used-in-critical-business-processes)). **Proposed:** short-lived single-use codes, shared limits on attempts/resends, and Cloud account-security SMS separate from venue messaging allowances so an exhausted venue messaging allowance cannot block sign-in. This is no unlimited-send promise.
