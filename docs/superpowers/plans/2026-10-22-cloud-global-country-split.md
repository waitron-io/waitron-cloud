# P3: Country accounts and a separate worldwide operator layer

> **PLAN PUBLICATION ONLY.** This replaces the earlier global-customer-identity plan for the owner's 24 October decisions. Publish and land the documentation, then have the driver ask for owner PLAN approval. Approval of the plan and explicit P3 build authority are separate gates. Nothing below authorises implementation, migrations, live schema, provider calls, deployment, spending or customer mail.

**Goal:** You sign in at `es.waitron.io` and manage your Spanish organisation, recover your account or start a new box recovery while global is absent. Each customer organisation has exactly one Cloud owner in its country. The same person operating in two countries has two separate accounts.

**Architecture:** Keep customer accounts and venue services in the country database. Create a clean, minimal database for the separate worldwide operator identity and console; do not export customer data into it. Country APIs authenticate customers locally and make final operator decisions locally. The apex sends visitors to their country; direct country access works without it.

## 1. Decisions and authority boundaries

All customer accounts, credentials, factors, passkeys, sessions, signup, sign-in, password reset, account recovery, organisations, legal-business/profile data, plans, customer alerts, account quotas and customer security records stay **COUNTRY**. So do venues, installations, pairing, remote access, backups and keys, monitoring, support grants, service consent, usage and fiscal records. Keep these relationships in one database; do not create global customer profiles, signed customer permits, membership projections, entitlement copies or cross-country customer revocation barriers.

Global keeps worldwide operator identity, its own security state, the console shell and apex routing only. It holds no customer or venue data, including identifiers, domains, account profiles, grants, backup catalogues, incident details, usage, country responses, action bodies/digests, logs, caches, queues or backups containing them. The operator's browser may read country responses directly into memory. The global server never proxies, aggregates or retains them. Worldwide reporting needs a later privacy/data-scope decision before any export.

Recommend country billing because customer self-service must not depend on global availability. Billing, prices, payment providers and any global billing/reporting are neither implemented nor approved by this plan. Existing test-plan policy and local allowance accounting remain country-owned; no new cross-country allowance service is introduced.

Customer ownership grants no platform-operator, support-recipient, fiscal, writer-admission or emergency powers. Keep every existing independent grant and final security check. Do not change fiscal algorithms, immutable fiscal records, signed bytes, hash chains or golden expectations. If a proposed owner or operator adapter cannot preserve a fiscal authority assertion, stop that affected slice for **separate owner review**, retaining the strict assertion. Do not implement a replacement fiscal actor contract here.

Preserve production §3's action-by-action permissions and routine-work distinction. P4 controls initiation; its two launch exceptions do not start hosted writers. Preserve §9's current A1018 direction: controlled final-copy/filing evidence or the freshly authenticated authorised person's explicit emergency declaration, source restrictions, sole M2 AEAT responsibility after either takeover, same-M2 retries, fresh target fiscal identity, fiscal-only return and the visible business gap. M1 returns originals, all outcomes and uncertainties without AEAT contact. There is no emergency predecessor ownership hold; temporary per-chain apply pauses and unresolved filing-case holds remain. The design was approved on 24 October at Waitron `060966c2b3e9203acf0849042814997307cae463`; formats, compatible A947 amendments, separate A1018 build authority and runtime acceptance remain open. An outage, Cloud ownership or operator approval supplies none of those missing authorities. Independently selected cold restore keeps its existing source/key/operation/sole-recovery and accepted-tail-loss checks; it never becomes an automatic fallback from failed handover.

P1 owns Kubernetes packaging and its real deployment proof; P2 owns provider infrastructure; P4 owns actions; P5 waves; P6 hosting/handover; P7 regional recovery. Spain remains `eu-south-2`, encrypted secondary copies Paris `eu-west-3`; global is separate in Frankfurt `eu-central-1`. P7 owns usable custody/database copies, the global second-region choice and D101/legal/provider qualification. No non-EU customer data is assigned to Frankfurt by default.

## 3. Single-owner authority and future handover

Propose `country_organisation_owners(organisation_id PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES organisations, account_id REFERENCES cloud_accounts, revision bigint CHECK >0)` as the country authority. One account may own several organisations; each organisation has exactly one owner. Country is fixed by the database scope, not a caller-supplied country field. Composite legal-business/organisation and venue/organisation relationships remain intact.

During the disabled additive stage, legacy memberships remain the old runtime's truth. At stopped activation, reconcile each organisation to **exactly one** existing `is_owner` account, with valid account/FK/country bindings. Zero owners, multiple owners, incompatible business-country data or unresolved privilege bindings refuse activation pending separate resolution. Never choose the first, oldest, enabled or most active owner. Non-owner legacy rows may remain internally for history and compatibility; they confer no split customer access. No public invitation, member, manager or second-owner lifecycle is added.

After activation, the new owner row is authoritative. A database constraint trigger ensures every customer organisation has exactly one row at commit; a partial unique index prevents two legacy `is_owner` rows; compatibility triggers require the active legacy owner row to match the new authority. Customer signup writes organisation, sole owner and required compatibility row atomically. Prevent direct application writes that bypass this invariant. Keep `can_register_venues` as an additional existing requirement for registration, not a new owner grant. Preserve legacy behavioral tests in legacy mode and add strict country-mode cases for formerly representable non-owner rows.

Proposed registration adapter in `packages/registration/src/global-country/customer-authority.ts`:

- `resolveCustomer(client, token, {csrfToken?, touch?}): Promise<Session>` delegates to existing country `resolveSession`.
- `requireCustomerOwner(client, accountId, organisationId, need:'read'|'owner'|'register'): Promise<{revision:number}>` requires the exact current sole owner, enabled account and existing operation-specific gates; `register` additionally requires the legacy registration flag. It is unavailable in legacy mode, which retains its original predicates.
- `recheckCustomer(client, token, proof, {organisationId, ownerRevision}): Promise<void>` repeats session and owner checks after waits and before returning private data/committing. Use the same country transaction, never a global call or cached profile.

Keep lock ordering consistent with each inspected store; do not introduce a universal wrapper that reverses existing locks. No SQL transaction spans DNS, SMTP, object transfer or other network work. Bound external preparation to the existing operation/target/revision, then repeat local authority checks at final commit. A lost reply resumes the same operation; reading its saved result requires current authority. Persist no reusable raw secret in an ordinary result cache.

**Future priority-5 handover requirement, not a P3 endpoint:** a freshly authenticated current owner initiates a country-scoped transfer to a separately verified recipient. Bind acceptance to the organisation, exact old/new account identities, owner revision and expiring single-use operation. Completion replaces exactly one owner atomically; there is never a second active owner. Immediately revoke the former owner's organisation access, pending ownership operations, relevant recovery/replacement approvals, support grants, relay/translation consent and queued alert eligibility derived from that ownership. End affected management sessions, invalidate stale reset/enrolment/acceptance paths that could restore organisation authority, and require fresh recipient authentication. Do not disable unrelated organisations or machine services merely because ownership changes.

Serialize completion with customer operations and account recovery. Final revision/identity/time checks follow waits; an old approval or uncertain response cannot complete against a new owner. A cancelled, expired or unfinished transfer leaves the current owner unchanged and gives the recipient no access. Retries are bound to the same operation; changed recipients/conflicting transfers refuse. Pending flows need enumeration resistance, shared attempt/rate limits, durable audit, notifications to independently verified channels and protection against replay, mailbox compromise and recovery racing a transfer. Lost-owner recovery must establish separately agreed evidence and abuse controls, never automatic support takeover, email-match authority or an operator override. Priority 5 owns exact endpoints, evidence, waiting/notification policy, account-session consequences and consent renewal; P3 supplies no public transfer/recovery primitive for this lifecycle. Until that design and its independent fiscal/Waitron checks are accepted, account ownership replacement remains unavailable.

## 4. Separate operator authority

Customer identity stays local and needs no new signed protocol. The separate global operator uses distinct IDs, credentials/factors, sessions, keys and cookie origin, with no implicit link even for matching email. Bootstrap that identity cleanly through explicit local fixture/configuration; never copy customer accounts, customer `platform_permissions` or customer ciphertext. Initial global tables contain only operator/security/rate-limit state. Operator provisioning/reset delivery uses separately scoped custody and a local test transport; no global customer mail worker or customer grant is inferred.

Replace the old `ControlScope`, `ControlClaims`, `ControlTrust`, `GlobalControl`, customer session exchange, cross-country customer revocation barrier and `HumanAuthority.membership` design with a **strictly operator-only** interface in new `packages/identity/src/operator-control.ts` and registration `src/global-country/operator-*.ts`:

`OperatorClaims={version:1, issuer:string, audience:string, country:string, keyId:string, operatorId:string, sessionId:string, permission:'cloud.actions.owner', authorityRevision:number, recoveryGeneration:number, nonce:string, issuedAt:number, expiresAt:number}`. Strings representing IDs are validated UUIDs, country is uppercase, revisions/generations are positive safe integers and times are Unix milliseconds. No organisation/customer/venue/cell identifier, action body or target digest enters global. The country prepares a random nonce and keeps the exact local operation, action, scope and body digest itself. Permission is the single closed initial allowlist entry; support needs its own separately qualified adapter and allowlist before any use.

`signOperator(claims,privateKey):SignedEnvelope`, `verifyOperator(envelope,trust,{issuer,audience,country},now):OperatorClaims`, and `OperatorAdmission.run(envelope,binding,work):Promise<T>` are proposed exports. `binding={operationId,kind,scope,bodyDigest}` is country-owned with closed operation kinds from P4 fixtures. `work` receives the same transaction's client and an opaque, client-bound operator handle; it cannot authenticate a customer route. Country additionally requires the exact configured operator ID plus current `cloud_action_authorities` grant, country/cell/action scope and final expiry/revision checks. No membership or invented organisation is required. P3 contract tests use a minimal fixture; they do not import unbuilt P4. Production uses an explicit country grant reader, unavailable until P4's accepted adapter exists; no fixture grant reader is selected by runtime fallback.

`OperatorAdmission.prepare(binding):Promise<{nonce:string;expiresAt:number}>` saves the immutable operation/kind/scope/body digest, current country recovery generation and a random nonce for at most 60 seconds. Preparation reveals no private result and grants no authority. Admission loads this saved binding, compares the caller's binding, then binds its operator/session once. `work(client,handle)` uses that exact SQL client. The handle expires when its callback ends; a different client, nested transaction or later reuse refuses. Final authority, the business mutation, nonce consumption, safe result identity and audit commit or roll back together.

P4's future patch must add supplied-client cores `proposeWithClient(client,handle,input)`, `decideWithClient(client,handle,input)`, `getWithClient(client,handle,scope,id)`, `pageWithClient(client,handle,scope,cursor,limit)`, `catalogueWithClient(client,handle,scope)` and `closeWithClient(client,handle,scope,input)`. Their return types remain P4's current methods; `handle` replaces its reusable token/CSRF arguments after admission has checked them. `ActionConsole` and incident HTTP adapters call these cores inside `OperatorAdmission.run`; explicit legacy pool-owning wrappers remain separate. For incident/other external preparation, finish bounded network reads before admission, then recheck their bound evidence inside it. A lost response keeps the original operation/result identity. Returning an existing private result requires a **new** prepared/admitted nonce with current authority for that same operation and binding; an expired or consumed original envelope cannot read it or execute again.

Use separate Ed25519 keys, canonical domain-separated bytes, canonical Base64url, fixed field order and strict extra-field rejection, following existing `installation-proof.ts` primitives. Cap permits at 60 seconds and the original operator session idle/absolute deadline; one nonce binds one operation, changed replay conflicts, saved private results require fresh authority. Reject wrong issuer/audience/country/key/version/signature, future issuance, expiry equality, oversize payload (>8192 decoded bytes), wrong key type and noncanonical encoding. Pin at most eight public keys with validity/revocation and monotonic trust generation. No network key discovery or fallback to customer cookies. Stage verification keys before signer rotation; remove a compromised key under the country admission lock. Missing/stale clock-health evidence (>60 seconds) or drift over 5 seconds refuses fresh **operator** permits, without changing country customer or machine clock rules. P1 must qualify this proposed clock adapter.

Operator revocation has an explicit limit: offline verification cannot discover an unseen global change. Global first closes the operator/session/permission epoch and denies issuance. Signed identity-only revocation messages to configured countries fence outstanding nonce admissions under local locks and obtain durable ACKs; global reports completion only after all relevant ACKs. A disconnected country leaves revocation visibly pending, with old permits bounded by their actual deadline. Country grant/key withdrawal uses its separate explicit operator/deployment authority locally; this creates no customer or emergency override. Lock waits require fresh time/authority before commit; an ACK waits for previously admitted local transactions. Already admitted effects remain facts. No two-phase commit or SQL transaction spans transport. Country grants/revocation/results and P4 command history never move global. Persist issuer/country/operator/session/epoch/nonce/receipt state only in global; reject venue-bearing error details.

P4 decisions and immutable dispatch authorisations additionally retain `operatorAuthority={operatorId,permission:'cloud.actions.owner',authorityRevision,recoveryGeneration}`. Permission removal or operator disable installs a country fence for this authority, not merely its unused nonces. Both final dispatch and the recipient's durable admission check this local fence after waits, without global SQL. Country ACK follows serialization with recipient admission: no previously queued, unadmitted command can cross that boundary after the fence completes. An effect admitted earlier stays a fact and reconciles through the same command/effect identity. Session logout closes fresh session admission; it does not by itself cancel durable approvals. Permission/key withdrawal and restore have their separately explicit durable-authority fences. Preserve P4's shorter applicable decision/grant/evidence deadlines; a 60-second request permit does not renew a 900-second decision.

Use a separate domain for signed `OperatorFence={version:1,issuer,country,operatorId,kind:'session'|'permission'|'operator'|'restore',subjectId,epoch,recoveryGeneration,exchangeId}` and a country-signed ACK binding its exact digest and installed epoch/generation. All IDs are UUIDs, enums/fields are closed and times/deadlines follow the control bounds above. Pin the country transport key and expected issuer; browser callers cannot install a fence. Persist the immutable exchange before sending; equal epoch/same digest retries return the same ACK, changed duplicates and backwards/gapped generations refuse. Install the relevant nonce/dispatch fence atomically; ACK loss resumes that exchange, never fabricates completion.

Global outage may delay fresh operator sign-in/issue and discretionary ask-first work. It cannot gate customer signup/sign-in, activity, security changes, recovery, consent, plans, quotas, new box recovery, captures or venue services. Do not require global refresh or periodic global health in country API readiness, workers or maintenance. Operator permits are not customer permits or autonomous service credentials.
