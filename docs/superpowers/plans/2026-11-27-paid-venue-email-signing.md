# Paid venue email signing in Cloud · slice 2b

In local tests, an operator can run `venue-mail:signing <venue-id>` to set up, check or retire signing for a test venue. Rerun after DNS or Amazon Simple Email Service (SES) updates, a sender reset or an accepted transfer. In this slice the portal tick still confirms only that the alias points at the requested name. Setting up signing when the owner saves the address and showing signing status in the portal belong to later slice 2b work. Slice 2c owns switching the staff sender and falling back to the Waitron address. [Cloud plan](2026-11-25-paid-venue-email-cloud.md#2b-show-verified-domain-records) · [Domain records plan](2026-11-25-paid-venue-email-domain-records.md)

## PR 1. Create, check and retire signing

1. Add `venue-mail-signing{,-cli}.ts` and a package script in `packages/remote-access`. Follow the registration cleanup command's one-shot pattern. Reuse `readVenuePermissions` and the bounded CNAME lookup. Move the active-installation predicate and alias builder from `account-http/src/venue-email.ts` into `remote-access` for both callers. Before claiming a domain, require and recheck the current Connected or Hosted test choice and its exact alias to `<selector>.mail.<suffix>`.
2. Append registration migration `089_venue_mail_signing.sql` in `migrate.ts`. Keep one row per venue and a unique normalised domain in the provider scope. Store organisation, selector, public key, scope and creation tag. Preserve the claim after venue deletion so cleanup can finish. Release a claim only if its saved tag still matches. Keep provider calls outside venue transactions.
3. Add `ses-signing.ts`, the SES SDK dependency and the generated lockfile. Document `CLOUD_VENUE_MAIL_{SES_REGION,DNS_ZONE_ID,TEST_DOMAINS}` in `.env.example`. Require an allowlisted test domain and a Route 53 zone serving `CLOUD_VENUE_MAIL_DNS_SUFFIX`. Assumption. The command is the only writer for those identities and records. Save a fresh 2048-bit RSA public key and tag before either provider write. Create SES first with the exact selector and in-memory private key so a definite SES rejection leaves no TXT record to clean up. After matching SES readback, use mail-only Route 53 `CREATE` in `route53-dns.ts` to publish a single TXT value at TTL 60 with quoted chunks of up to 255 characters. Both creates refuse duplicates. Wait for `INSYNC` within the existing deadline. Preserve certificate rules.
4. Extend `dns-lookup.ts` with bounded TXT resolution. Verify the exact alias and one matching TXT record with its chunks joined, the SES tag and selector, enabled external signing, DKIM `SUCCESS` and SES sending verification. Recheck the current organisation, address, selector and paid installation before reporting verified. Mailbox edits on the same domain reuse the key. Require exact identity proof here and in 2c, even when SES inherits permission from a parent domain. [SES identity rules](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/creating-identities.html) · [Identity read](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/APIReference-V2/API_GetEmailIdentity.html)
5. Turn off automatic write retries. On each rerun read SES and the exact normalised Route 53 name and type. Resume on a match. Keep the original key and tag while the outcome of its create is uncertain. Release the claim when SES definitively refuses the create. Rerun by venue to retire a stale claim even if its venue is gone. Check the SES tag before deleting. Once SES is absent, delete only the exact saved TXT with its original TTL and chunks. Read back in Route 53 before releasing the claim. If cleanup starts with neither record visible, hold the claim for an operator to reconcile. A timed-out create may still appear. Leave a foreign identity or record alone.
